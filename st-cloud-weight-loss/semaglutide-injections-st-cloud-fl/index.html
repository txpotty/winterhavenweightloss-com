    How Semaglutide Works in St. Cloud

    Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist, a lab-made version of a gut hormone your body releases after eating. The signal reaches appetite centers in the brain, while the stomach holds food a little longer. Cravings usually ease. Because the molecule is built to resist breakdown, it lasts about a week in the body, which is why a single injection covers seven full days. It also lowers post-meal blood sugar, which is why Ozempic treats type 2 diabetes.

    Who Semaglutide Is For

    Adults with a BMI of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with a weight-related condition, are the usual candidates. Common qualifying conditions include high blood pressure, high cholesterol, prediabetes and sleep apnea. It is not for type 1 diabetes. Wegovy also carries an FDA approval to lower the risk of heart attack and stroke in adults with heart disease and excess weight. The provider weighs all of this against your full health history before deciding.

    Dosing and the Weekly Schedule in St. Cloud

    The standard Wegovy ladder starts at 0.25 mg weekly, then climbs through four more monthly steps before reaching 2.4 mg. Each step usually lasts four weeks. Pick a weekday and stick with it, morning or night, fed or fasting. The shot goes under the skin of the belly or thigh, and moving the spot each week cuts down on redness and small bumps. Five days is the window for a late dose, and after that you wait for the next scheduled shot.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Weeks one to four at 0.25 mg are a warm-up, and some people feel almost nothing beyond a slightly smaller appetite. The first 48 hours after each injection are when queasiness is most likely. The 1 mg step is a common turning point. Around month three, portion sizes tend to drop on their own and late-night snacking fades. In STEP 1, weight loss kept building through week 60 before leveling off, so the first eight weeks are only a small part of the picture.

    Side Effects of Semaglutide in St. Cloud

    Nausea, diarrhea, constipation and vomiting are the most reported side effects, usually mild and tied to dose increases. Slower, smaller meals with less fried food make a real difference. Hydrate well. Less common risks include pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, kidney problems from dehydration and low blood sugar when semaglutide is paired with insulin or a sulfonylurea. The label carries a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents.

    Eligibility and Screening

    The intake asks about a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2, which rules semaglutide out entirely. Your provider also looks for past pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, diabetic retinopathy, severe stomach emptying problems and any history of an eating disorder before writing a prescription. Pregnancy rules it out. Recent labs such as an A1c or a kidney panel help but are not always required.

    Semaglutide Compared With Related Options in St. Cloud

    Tirzepatide, sold as Zepbound, produced larger average weight loss than semaglutide in the SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head trial, about 20 percent versus 14 percent at 72 weeks. Semaglutide has the longer track record and the cardiovascular approval. A daily semaglutide tablet exists for patients who would rather not inject. Shots are simpler for most. Compounded semaglutide uses the same ingredient but is not FDA-approved.
